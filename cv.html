ABOUT

"I study why India's welfare programmes fail at the level of human behaviour — connecting what policy intends with why people actually respond the way they do."

I work at the intersection of development economics, behavioural economics, and public policy — as a Research Assistant at IIM Ahmedabad, where I build the policy databases that underpin national climate and welfare policy tracking. My independent research focuses on why welfare schemes are delivered but not sustained.

My throughline is simple: a scheme that reaches a household is not the same as a scheme that is used by one. I bring an economist's rigour — regression diagnostics, decomposition methods, structured QA — to the messier, more human question of why people accept, ignore, or abandon what policy gives them.

RESEARCH

Working Paper

Delivered but Deserted: Behavioral Determinants of Non-Occupancy in PMAY-Urban

India's flagship urban housing scheme has delivered millions of units — yet a significant share sit vacant after handover. This paper argues the gap is not administrative, but behavioural: a formal decomposition of why beneficiaries fail to sustain occupancy.

Method: Oaxaca–Blinder decomposition separating observable household characteristics from behavioural response, grounded in 15-source literature base.
